apoio_EN26A: When a bank rejects a loan application, the customer usually wants to know why. If the decision was made by an algorithm, however, the answer may be hard to obtain. Some companies argue that explaining their models would expose trade secrets; others admit that even their own engineers cannot fully trace how a particular result was reached.
Regulators have responded in different ways. Several data protection laws now give individuals the right to request a review of decisions taken solely on the basis of automated processing. Such a right, though, is only meaningful if people are told, in plain language, which factors were considered and how much each of them weighed.
Transparency does not require publishing source code. What matters is that a person affected by a decision can understand it well enough to challenge it. Without that, accountability becomes a promise that nobody is able to verify.
