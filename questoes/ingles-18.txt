apoio_EN18A: Regulatory sandboxes have become a popular tool for agencies that want to encourage innovation without abandoning their duty to protect the public. In a sandbox, a company is allowed to test a new product with real customers for a limited period, under close supervision and with some rules temporarily relaxed. In return, it must share data about how the product performs and report any harm promptly.
Supporters argue that sandboxes help regulators learn before they write rules, rather than after problems have spread. Critics, however, worry that participation may give selected firms an unfair head start, and that “temporary” exemptions can quietly become permanent if no one reviews them. Both concerns can be addressed, at least in part, through transparent selection criteria and clear exit conditions.
Ultimately, a sandbox is only as good as the lessons drawn from it. If the regulator does not publish what it has learned, competitors and the public are left in the dark, and the experiment benefits only those who were inside it.
