apoio_EN22A: Governments around the world are pushing online platforms to verify the age of their users. The goal is easy to support: children should not be exposed to content and features designed for adults. The method, however, is far more contested.
Most age checks rely on one of three approaches: asking users to upload an identity document, estimating age from a selfie, or inferring it from behaviour. Each of them collects information that would not otherwise be needed, and some of it, such as facial images, is highly sensitive. Critics warn that a measure meant to protect minors could end up creating vast new databases about everyone.
Supporters reply that the technology has improved and that well-designed systems can confirm whether someone is over a certain age without revealing who they are. Whether that promise holds will depend less on the software than on the rules that limit how long the data is kept and who may access it.
