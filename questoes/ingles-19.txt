apoio_EN19A: Many apps ask for far more information than they need. A flashlight app that requests access to your contacts, or a game that wants your precise location, is collecting data simply because it can. Privacy regulators call the opposite approach data minimisation: organisations should gather only what is necessary for a stated purpose and keep it no longer than needed.
The principle sounds obvious, yet it often clashes with business incentives. Data that seems useless today might become valuable tomorrow, so companies are tempted to store everything “just in case”. Interface design can make matters worse. So-called dark patterns, such as pre-ticked boxes or a bright “Accept all” button placed next to a barely visible “Manage options” link, nudge users towards choices they would not otherwise make.
Regulators have started to treat such designs as a form of manipulation rather than mere bad taste. Consent obtained through tricks, they argue, is not genuinely free, and data collected in this way may have to be deleted.
