apoio_EN27A: Most websites now greet visitors with a banner asking them to accept cookies. In theory, this gives users control over how their browsing is tracked. In practice, many banners are designed to steer people towards the option that benefits the website. The “Accept all” button is large and brightly coloured, while the option to refuse is hidden behind several clicks.
Regulators call these tricks “dark patterns”. They do not lie outright, but they exploit predictable habits: people in a hurry tend to click whatever is easiest. Some authorities have already fined companies for making refusal harder than acceptance, arguing that consent obtained this way is not freely given.
A fair design is not difficult to imagine. Refusing should take no more effort than accepting, and the purpose of each type of cookie should be explained in plain words. Until that becomes the norm, most “choices” online will remain choices in name only.
