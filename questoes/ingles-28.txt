apoio_EN28A: For years, many companies treated personal data as an asset to be kept indefinitely. Storage was cheap, and nobody knew which information might prove useful later. That attitude is changing. Data protection laws now require organisations to define how long they keep each type of record and to delete what is no longer needed.
Deletion, however, is harder than it sounds. Copies of the same file may sit in backups, in old servers and in the systems of service providers. Unless a company knows where its data actually is, it cannot honestly promise to erase it.
For this reason, experts recommend mapping data flows before writing a retention policy. A policy that lists deadlines but ignores where information is stored may look good on paper, yet it rarely works in practice.
