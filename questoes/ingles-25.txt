apoio_EN25A: Collecting more data than necessary may seem harmless, but every extra field in a form is also an extra risk. Data that a company does not hold cannot be stolen, leaked or misused. This simple idea lies behind the principle of data minimisation, which appears in data protection laws around the world.
In practice, minimisation means asking a few questions before collecting anything: what is this information for, how long will it be needed, and could the same goal be achieved with less? A delivery service, for example, needs an address, but probably not the customer's date of birth.
Minimisation also applies after collection. Records should be deleted or anonymised once they are no longer needed, rather than kept “just in case”. Companies that adopt this discipline often find that it reduces storage costs and simplifies compliance, in addition to protecting the people whose data they handle.
