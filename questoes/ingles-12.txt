apoio_EN12A: Most people click “Accept all” within seconds of seeing a cookie banner. Regulators once hoped that these pop-ups would give users meaningful control over their data. Instead, many users now experience them as an obstacle between them and the content they want, and they agree to almost anything just to make the banner disappear.
Researchers call this “consent fatigue”. It is not simply laziness: when every website asks the same question in slightly different ways, and when refusing takes several clicks while accepting takes one, the design itself nudges people toward agreement. In such cases, consent may be formally valid but practically meaningless.
Some authorities have responded by requiring a “Reject all” button as visible as the “Accept all” option. Others argue that the real solution lies elsewhere: fewer trackers by default, so that users are asked for consent only when it truly matters.
