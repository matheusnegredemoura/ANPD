apoio: When children go online, they rarely read the terms they accept, and their parents often do not either. For years, companies treated a box ticked by a twelve-year-old as meaningful consent. Lawmakers in several countries have now rejected that fiction. Instead of asking users to declare their age, platforms are increasingly required to verify it, and services likely to be accessed by minors must be designed with their best interests in mind.
Age assurance, however, raises its own privacy concerns. Asking every user to upload an identity document may protect children while exposing adults to new risks of data breaches. Regulators therefore favour methods that confirm only what is necessary, such as whether a user is over a certain age, without revealing who that user is.
Designing such systems is not merely a technical challenge. It requires deciding who bears the cost, how mistakes will be corrected, and which authority will hold platforms to account when they fail.
