apoio_EN20A: Several countries now require online platforms to check users’ ages before showing them certain content. The goal is easy to defend: children should not stumble upon violent or sexual material while scrolling. The methods, however, are more controversial. Asking every user to upload an identity document creates a large database of sensitive information, which could itself become a target for criminals.
Some companies prefer to estimate age from a selfie, using software that does not store the image. Others rely on checks performed by a trusted third party, which simply confirms whether the user is over a certain age without revealing who they are. Each option involves a trade-off between accuracy, cost and privacy.
Critics also point out that determined teenagers will always find ways around these barriers. For that reason, many experts argue that age checks should be only one layer of protection, combined with safer default settings and better education for both parents and children.
