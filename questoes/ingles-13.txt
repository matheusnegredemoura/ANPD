apoio_EN13A: When a child creates an account on a gaming platform, she rarely reads the privacy notice, and even when she does, the language is seldom written for her. Regulators have increasingly argued that the burden should not rest on the child at all. Instead, platforms are expected to design their services so that the most protective settings are switched on by default.
This approach, often called privacy by default, shifts responsibility from the user to the company. Location sharing, for instance, should be off unless a parent decides otherwise, and features that push children to play longer, such as endless streaks and random rewards, are coming under growing scrutiny.
Critics warn that age verification, a common companion to these rules, may itself require collecting more data than before. The challenge, therefore, is to protect children without turning every login into an identity check.
