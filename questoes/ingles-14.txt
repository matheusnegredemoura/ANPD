apoio_EN14A: Regulatory sandboxes have become a popular tool for authorities that want to keep pace with technology. In a sandbox, a company is allowed to test an innovative product with real users for a limited period, under close supervision and with some rules temporarily relaxed.
Supporters argue that this arrangement benefits both sides. Firms can learn whether their ideas are viable before investing heavily, while regulators gain first-hand knowledge of new business models instead of reacting to them only after problems emerge.
Critics, however, point out that sandboxes may favour well-connected companies and that the lessons learned are rarely made public. Unless the results are shared, they argue, the wider market gains little, and the sandbox risks becoming a privilege rather than a learning tool.
