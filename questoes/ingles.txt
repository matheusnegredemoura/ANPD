apoio: Data protection authorities around the world are being asked to do more with less. As artificial intelligence systems spread into hiring, lending and public services, regulators must decide not only whether personal data was collected lawfully, but also whether automated decisions can be explained to the people they affect.
Some authorities have responded by publishing guidance before issuing fines, on the assumption that most organisations want to comply but lack clear instructions. Others argue that guidance alone is toothless unless it is backed by credible enforcement. A middle path, often called responsive regulation, starts with dialogue and escalates to sanctions only when an organisation repeatedly ignores its obligations.
Critics warn, however, that this approach can favour large companies, which can afford lawyers to negotiate at every stage. Whatever the strategy, transparency remains essential: an authority that does not explain its priorities invites suspicion that its decisions are arbitrary.
