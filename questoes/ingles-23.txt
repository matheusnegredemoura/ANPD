apoio_EN23A: Many apps make it easy to sign up and surprisingly hard to leave. A subscription can be started with one tap, while cancelling it requires finding a hidden menu, answering several questions and resisting a last-minute discount offer. Designers call these tricks “dark patterns”: interface choices that steer users towards decisions they might not otherwise make.
Some dark patterns are subtle. A pre-ticked box that shares data with “partners”, a countdown timer that creates false urgency, or a button that says “No thanks, I don't like saving money” all exploit predictable human biases. Individually, each may seem harmless; together, they shape millions of choices every day.
Regulators have started to respond. Several jurisdictions now treat manipulative design as an unfair practice, and data protection authorities have warned that consent obtained through such tricks may not be valid. The challenge is drawing the line between persuasion, which is a normal part of marketing, and manipulation, which undermines free choice.
