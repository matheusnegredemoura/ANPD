apoio_EN24A: Governments are increasingly using chatbots to answer citizens' questions about taxes, benefits and public services. Supporters point out that the systems are available around the clock and can handle thousands of requests at once, freeing staff for more complex cases.
However, early experiences have revealed problems. Some chatbots have given confident but wrong answers about legal deadlines, and citizens who followed that advice lost benefits they were entitled to. Because the tools often sound authoritative, users may not realise they should double-check what they are told.
Experts recommend that public agencies clearly label automated assistants, keep a human channel open for those who need it, and regularly test the accuracy of the answers. Above all, an agency cannot outsource its responsibility: if its chatbot misleads a citizen, the agency remains accountable.
